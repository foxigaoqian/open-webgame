WHAT IS HEARTH AND HAMLET?

A city builder shaped around steady, incremental growth

Steam describes the game as a medieval citybuilder clicker with resource management, upgrade trees and relaxing idle play. The settlement starts around a campfire and expands as new systems become available.

START

Build a working settlement

Your first job is economic: collect essentials, assign population to useful work and turn basic materials into buildings. The official feature list specifically names food, wood, stone and gold among the resources your workers can gather.

GROW

Research permanent progress

Research unlocks more advanced buildings, upgrades, policies and stronger magic. That makes the technology tree more than a side menu: it is one of the main ways the settlement changes from a simple camp into a more capable kingdom.

RULE

Shape the kingdom with policy

Growth adds management choices. Official descriptions mention taxation, worker rules, city-guard drafts and other civic decisions, so expansion is not only about placing the next building; it also changes how the kingdom is governed.

BEGINNER GUIDE

What to prioritize in your first settlement

This guide stays at the system level so it remains useful without inventing undocumented build orders, hidden bonuses or exact ratios.

01

Stabilize the basic resource loop

Before chasing every unlock, make sure worker assignments cover the materials needed for the next wave of buildings and research. Hearth and Hamlet is incremental, so production capacity compounds: a weak foundation makes every later target feel slower.

02

Read the next unlock before spending

Research is a permanent progression layer. When two upgrades compete for the same resources, compare what each one unlocks next. An upgrade that opens a new production path, building tier or management tool can be more valuable than a small isolated gain.

03

Rebalance workers after major unlocks

A new building or technology can change which resource is the bottleneck. Revisit your worker distribution instead of leaving the early-game split untouched. The goal is not to maximize one number forever; it is to keep the next meaningful objective moving.

04

Treat policies as economic tools

Laws and policies affect how the kingdom operates, including income, morale and defense-related choices mentioned by the developer. Read them as part of the economy rather than flavor text, especially when the settlement's priorities change.

05

Use trade to widen the resource base

The official game description explicitly frames trade as a way to grow beyond what local terrain provides. When trade opens, evaluate it as another production channel rather than assuming every shortage must be solved by adding more local workers.

06

Let idle progression work for you

The game is designed around a relaxing incremental rhythm. Queue your next sensible objective, put workers on the resources that support it and avoid turning every short wait into frantic clicking. Planning and patience are part of the intended loop.
